## Home-country business press

Rules: `SKILL.md` → "Home-country business press" (MANDATORY), including the closure check and the proof-of-life rule.

### The udon house no local source knew about

A search for a dedicated udon house in a Spanish city ran three rounds — Spanish editorial, Guía Repsol, ComerJapones, Japanese expat Q&A boards, two Reddit threads, and a menu-phrase sweep — and all of them converged on "this city has no udon-ya". It was stated as verified. It was wrong. A listed Japanese noodle chain with a few hundred restaurants at home had opened a noodle house there. Nikkei reported it on opening day, quoting the format directly: soba and udon at the core, sushi only under consideration. Not one Spanish source, and not ComerJapones, ever mentioned it. The expat Q&A thread predated the opening.

What it shows: four source families agreeing only proves those four families do not cover the venue. Say "I did not find one, in these sources" — never "there is none".

### The closure that was never checked

Same case: the noodle house was found via Nikkei's opening-day report. It had closed in less than a year — reported by the Japanese regional business press and listed in the operator's own IR closures. It was recommended as a live venue anyway, because the search queried the opening and never the closure, while an Uber Eats page and a menu-aggregator page were both still up. The closure only came to light when the map link resolved to an empty search.

What it shows: a business-press hit proves an opening, not a current venue. Run the closure query, and check that the Google Maps pin resolves before the recommendation ships.

### The brand name that drowns the search

"Udon" in Spain returned the 81-location UDON Asian Food chain on every query, in every language (location count as observed in 2026). When a common dish word is also a local brand, keyword search is structurally dead. Go to the business wires and to menu-phrase searches (`kitsune udon`, `kake udon`) instead.

## Diaspora-language Q&A boards

Rules: `SKILL.md` → "Diaspora-language Q&A boards" (MANDATORY).

### Why the Q&A format matters more than the platform

These are people answering a specific question from a specific person — not writing for an audience, not being paid, not performing taste. Answers name streets and dishes, and they contradict each other openly. This layer routinely surfaces venues that are invisible to every local-language source, and those can end up being the ones recommended.

### The dish, not the verdict

A Japanese blogger wrote of one venue's kitsune udon 「出汁が惜しい」 — "the dashi falls short" — and that single clause outweighed a candidate's high Google Maps rating, hundreds of reviews and a ComerJapones listing. Nobody writing in Spanish tastes dashi and reports on it. That is why the rule says to search for the dish name in the cuisine's script (うどん, 天ぷら, 出汁) rather than the venue name.

### A spread, not a consensus

In one thread a resident called a venue "正に日本の味" while a Reddit local dismissed the same place on ownership alone. Both were reported; the ownership objection is not a food objection.

### Old threads, closed venues

A single search turned up three Reddit/locotabi-recommended venues that were permanently closed. The boards do not mark closures, so every venue from them needs its own open-status check.

## Eater — dated coverage notes

- 31 of Eater's 47 international lists had never been touched since commission.
